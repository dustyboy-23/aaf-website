<h1>Grok Bot vs Claude Cowork vs Gemini Spark: Which Desktop AI Agent Should You Use?</h1>

<p class="lede">Three desktop AI agents launched in August 2026 at very different price points and architectures. Claude Cowork starts at $20/month and wins for file-creation and local filesystem work. Gemini Spark starts at $19.99/month and dominates Gmail and Google Workspace automation. Grok Bot requires at least $120/month via a SuperGrok Heavy or Cursor subscription and is the only one that keeps running after you close your laptop.</p>

<p>I have been testing all three as they shipped this month. Here is a straight comparison -- what each one actually does, who should use it, and where each one falls short.</p>

<h2>What is Grok Bot and how does it work?</h2>

<p>Grok Bot is xAI's always-on AI agent. Each bot gets its own persistent cloud computer -- a virtual machine with a browser, filesystem, and terminal -- that operates your apps through their real interfaces without requiring integrations. It launched in beta on August 11, 2026, with a 7-day free trial opening to the public on August 21.</p>

<p>The persistent cloud computer model is the key architectural difference. Grok Bot keeps running after you close your laptop because the work happens on xAI's servers, not your device. You are the supervisor -- the bot surfaces when it needs approval and continues autonomously otherwise.</p>

<p>The catch is pricing. Grok Bot is not a standalone product. Access is bundled into subscriptions you are probably already paying for something else: SuperGrok Heavy at roughly $300/month, Cursor Ultra at $200/month, or Cursor Teams at $120/month (Standard seats). There is no cheap entry point. If you are not already on one of those tiers, Grok Bot is a $120+ commitment per month.</p>

<p>Platform coverage is broad: macOS, iOS, Windows, and Linux launched at beta with Android listed as coming soon. Multiple bots can share context and coordinate on overlapping tasks, which is the early multi-agent story xAI is building toward.</p>

<h2>What is Claude Cowork and how does it work?</h2>

<p>Claude Cowork is Anthropic's desktop AI agent that works directly in your files. It reached general availability on April 9, 2026, included with every paid Claude plan starting at the Pro tier at $20/month. The Max 5x tier ($100/month) and Max 20x tier ($200/month) get higher usage limits and priority access.</p>

<p>Originally desktop-only, Anthropic expanded Cowork to web, iOS, and Android on July 7, 2026, and began rolling out cloud remote sessions (in beta, Max plan first) where tasks run on Anthropic's servers. The core model is Claude Opus 5 with a 1M-token context window -- enough to hold an entire large codebase or a year of documents in a single session.</p>

<p>Cowork grants Claude permission to a specific folder on your machine, then reads, edits, and creates files inside that boundary. Optional MCP connectors and a Chrome extension expand its reach into apps and the web. The Microsoft 365 integration is a first-class feature at Pro and up, which makes it a natural fit for teams running Excel, Word, and PowerPoint workflows.</p>

<p>The practical limit on the Pro tier is 3-5 heavy sessions per day before hitting usage caps. For document-heavy work that does not need 24/7 autonomy, the $20 entry point is hard to beat.</p>

<h2>What is Gemini Spark and how does it work?</h2>

<p>Gemini Spark is Google's 24/7 cloud AI agent, built to run continuously in the background connected to Gmail, Calendar, Google Docs, Sheets, Slides, and Drive through proper APIs. It launched as part of the Google AI Ultra plan at $99.99/month, then came down to the AI Pro tier at $19.99/month as of July 23, 2026. That price drop matters -- it makes Spark directly competitive with Claude Cowork on cost for casual use.</p>

<p>The distinguishing feature is the Daily Brief: every morning Spark distills your Gmail, Calendar, and recent Gemini sessions into a prioritized action plan with suggested next steps. For professionals who run their lives inside Google Workspace, that integration depth is genuinely difficult to replicate with any other tool.</p>

<p>Spark also connects to third-party services including Instacart, OpenTable, and Canva for task execution beyond Google''s own ecosystem. The underlying model is Gemini 3.5 with what Google calls the Antigravity harness for multi-step agentic work.</p>

<p>The geographic restriction is a real limitation: Spark is unavailable in the EEA, UK, Switzerland, and Nigeria as of August 2026. If you are outside the US, availability is not guaranteed.</p>

<h2>Side-by-side: pricing, architecture, and where each wins</h2>

<p>Here is where the three agents stand as of August 2026, with specific numbers rather than marketing framing.</p>

<p><strong>Pricing entry points:</strong> Claude Cowork at $20/month (Pro) -- the cheapest real entry. Gemini Spark at $19.99/month (AI Pro) -- essentially tied. Grok Bot at $120+/month via Cursor Teams -- the most expensive by a significant margin, and only accessible if you are already on a qualifying subscription.</p>

<p><strong>Where they live:</strong> Grok Bot runs entirely in xAI''s cloud on a dedicated virtual machine per task. Gemini Spark runs on Google''s servers with direct API connections to Workspace. Claude Cowork runs desktop-first with cloud remote sessions available on Max plans -- it was the only one with true local-file access from day one.</p>

<p><strong>File types:</strong> Claude Cowork handles Excel, Word, PowerPoint, and PDF natively. Gemini Spark is limited to Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides -- you need to work inside Google''s formats or convert. Grok Bot works through real app interfaces, so it can technically handle anything a human could click through, but reliability on complex multi-step workflows is unproven at the beta stage.</p>

<p><strong>Background operation:</strong> Grok Bot is the only one that keeps running after your device closes. Gemini Spark also runs in the cloud 24/7. Claude Cowork''s cloud remote sessions (Max plan, beta) can persist without your device, but standard Cowork requires your machine to be on.</p>

<h2>Which one should you actually use?</h2>

<p>The decision is less about which agent is better overall and more about which ecosystem your work already lives in.</p>

<p><strong>Use Claude Cowork if:</strong> your work involves creating files -- reports, decks, spreadsheets, code -- on a local machine. The $20 Pro tier is the most accessible serious AI agent on the market right now. The Microsoft 365 integration and 1M-token context window handle the workflows that trip up most competitors. If you are not on a Google-heavy stack, Cowork is the default recommendation.</p>

<p><strong>Use Gemini Spark if:</strong> Gmail and Google Workspace are where your real work happens. The API-native access to Calendar, Docs, and Sheets is meaningfully better than any screen-scraping alternative. The $19.99/month Pro tier makes it accessible -- though EEA and UK users cannot access it yet. The Daily Brief alone is worth it if your mornings currently start with inbox triage.</p>

<p><strong>Use Grok Bot if:</strong> you need a cloud agent that works across applications that do not have APIs or integrations -- or if you need continuous background operation without keeping your device on and you are already paying for a SuperGrok Heavy or Cursor subscription. The $120+ floor is too high for casual testing, and beta status means production reliability is unproven. Come back to this one in Q4 2026 once it ships out of beta.</p>

<h2>What is still missing from all three</h2>

<p>None of these agents solves the cross-tool workflow problem cleanly yet. Grok Bot''s cloud VM approach is architecturally the most general -- if it can click through real app UIs, it works anywhere -- but that generality comes with brittleness. UI-based automation breaks when apps update their layouts.</p>

<p>Claude Cowork is the most controllable and transparent of the three. You know exactly which folder it can access, which tools it has connected, and which model is running. That matters for anyone who needs an audit trail or works with sensitive documents. But the usage caps on the $20 tier are real friction for heavy users.</p>

<p>Gemini Spark''s API-native integrations are the most reliable for the specific apps it supports, but the Google ecosystem boundary is a hard wall. If your business runs on Microsoft 365 or anything outside Google''s stack, Spark has no path in.</p>

<p>The larger pattern: all three agents launched between April and August 2026, are in early product stages with meaningful limitations, and are improving fast. The right pick today may not be the right pick in six months. I would watch Grok Bot''s post-beta pricing and Claude Cowork''s cloud session rollout most closely -- those two have the most room to shift the market.</p>

<h2>FAQ</h2>

<h3>What is the cheapest way to get a desktop AI agent in 2026?</h3>
<p>Claude Cowork at $20/month (Claude Pro) and Gemini Spark at $19.99/month (Google AI Pro) are the two cheapest serious desktop AI agents available in August 2026. Both include real agentic capabilities -- file access and multi-step task execution -- not just chatbot features. Grok Bot requires at least $120/month via a Cursor Teams subscription.</p>

<h3>Can Grok Bot work while my computer is off?</h3>
<p>Yes. Grok Bot runs on a dedicated cloud virtual machine on xAI''s servers, so it keeps working after you close your laptop or power off your device. Gemini Spark also operates continuously in the cloud. Claude Cowork''s cloud remote sessions (Max plan, currently in beta) can persist without your device, but standard Cowork requires your machine to be running.</p>

<h3>Is Gemini Spark available outside the US?</h3>
<p>Gemini Spark is not available in the European Economic Area (EEA), UK, Switzerland, or Nigeria as of August 2026. It launched in the US with a promise to expand to other countries soon. Claude Cowork and Grok Bot are available globally.</p>

<h3>What file types can Claude Cowork handle that Gemini Spark cannot?</h3>
<p>Claude Cowork handles Excel, Word, PowerPoint, and PDF files natively. Gemini Spark is limited to Google Docs, Sheets, Slides, and Drive files within the Google ecosystem. If your work involves Microsoft 365 formats or local files, Claude Cowork is the better fit.</p>
